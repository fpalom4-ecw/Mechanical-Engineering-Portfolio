NASA Lunabotics

Testing field prototypes on simulated lunar terrain

Project Scope: Autonomous exploration rover development.

View Case Study

NASA's Lunabotics Challenge is a national competition where university teams design, build, and test rovers that excavate simulated lunar regolith to construct a berm, directly supporting NASA's Artemis program. It's highly competitive: only 50 of the universities that apply each year are accepted.

In 2026, the field got even tougher when top tier schools like MIT and Caltech joined for the first time.

Teams are scored on several deliverables, including a Systems Engineering Paper applying NASA's systems engineering process.

As mechanical lead for excavation and drivetrain, I ran weighted trade studies to guide key design decisions and led formal design reviews at every control gate to keep the project on track.

As team lead, I worked closely with our controls and electrical subteams on control logic and electrical enclosure layout, making sure mechanical decisions stayed compatible across disciplines.

As project manager, I led our weekly meetings and managed budget, schedule, and risk across the two semester project, keeping the team accountable from kickoff to competition.

Our team earned an Innovation Award in both 2024 and 2025. In 2024, we placed 10th after nearly missing qualifiers due to major code issues, but we pushed through and fixed it in time.

NASA judges were impressed enough to create a brand new award for us: the "Failure Is Not An Option" award.
